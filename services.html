Picking a Profile for Your Spring Hill Property

Subdivision homes: Standing seam is a favorite for a clean finish; 5V crimp offers a traditional Florida look.

Homes west toward Weeki Wachee and the coast: Owners closer to the Gulf often ask about coatings and fasteners suited to salt air; your installer can advise.

Larger lots and outbuildings: PBR is practical for workshops and storage buildings and can be color-matched to the house.

Shops along US-19: Light commercial roofs commonly use standing seam or PBR; the installer confirms what code requires.

Before You Sign: Questions for the Installer

Which profile works on my roof pitch? Will the old shingles be removed? What underlayment and fastening schedule meet code here? Does my HOA need to approve the color? How will vents, skylights and valleys be flashed? Ask for answers in writing.

Color Choices

White, light gray and tan reflect the most heat through a long Spring Hill summer. Sage green, bronze and charcoal blend in under pines and live oaks. If you have an HOA, bring its approved palette when you look at samples.
